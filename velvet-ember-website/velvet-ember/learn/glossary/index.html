Self-heating drinks glossary

The words you’ll meet when reading about self-heating cans, defined in a sentence or two.

By the Velvet Ember team. Published and updated 17 November 2026.

Exothermic reaction
A chemical reaction that releases heat. Self-heating cans use one to warm their contents.
Calcium oxide (quicklime)
A white solid that reacts with water and gives off heat. Velvet Ember’s design uses 69 g of food-grade calcium oxide.
Calcium hydroxide (slaked lime)
What calcium oxide becomes after reacting with water. In a self-heating can it stays sealed inside the heating module.
Thermo-module
The sealed heating unit in a self-heating can. In Velvet Ember it occupies the lower 190 ml chamber.
Heat-transfer cell
The sealed aluminium container that holds the reagents and passes heat through its wall into the drink.
Reagent bed
The layer of reacting material, here calcium oxide, inside the heating module.
Activation
The action that starts the heating reaction, such as pressing or twisting the base of the can.
Payload area
The part of the can that holds the drink. Velvet Ember’s is 230 ml.
Solid-state heater
A heating method that uses a reaction between solids rather than adding water. Used by some other self-heating can makers.
Ready-to-drink (RTD)
A beverage sold already prepared, so you don’t need to mix or brew it.
FSSAI
The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India, which regulates food safety and licensing in India.
