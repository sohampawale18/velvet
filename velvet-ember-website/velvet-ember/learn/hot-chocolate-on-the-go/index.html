Hot chocolate on the go: your options compared

Want a hot chocolate somewhere without a kitchen? Here’s how the main options compare, honestly, including where a self-heating can isn’t the best choice.

By the Velvet Ember team. Published and updated 17 November 2026.

If you can boil water before you leave, a flask of home-made hot chocolate is usually the cheapest option. If there’s hot water where you’re going, an instant sachet is lightest. A café gives you the freshest cup but needs one nearby. A self-heating can is for when none of those are available, and it costs more because the heater is built in.

Things people often get wrong about self-heating cans

“The whole can is drink”

No. Part of every self-heating can is the heater. Velvet Ember’s 420 ml can has a 230 ml drink section; the rest is the heating chamber.

“It heats instantly”

Heating takes some time, usually minutes. Different products state different times, and we’re validating Velvet Ember’s before publishing it.

“The chemicals go in the drink”

In a properly designed can, the reaction happens in a separately sealed chamber and only heat passes into the drink.

Which should you choose?

Pick a flask or sachet if you have access to hot water; they’re cheaper and create less packaging per drink. Consider a self-heating can for the times you genuinely can’t get hot water, like a long journey, an outdoor trip or a hostel with no kettle.
